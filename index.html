Formula Information

Theoretical Power Formula:

Pth = ρ × g × Q × h

  • Pth = Theoretical power (W or ft·lbf/s)
  • ρ / γ = Water density / specific weight (kg/m³ or lbf/ft³)
  • g = Gravity — 9.81 m/s² (SI only; cancels in Imperial)
  • Q = Flow rate (m³/s or ft³/s)
  • h = Head / height (m or ft)

Practical Power Formula:

Pa = μ × Pth

  • Pa = Actual available power (W or ft·lbf/s)
  • μ = Efficiency (typically 75% to 95%)

Annual Energy:

E [MWh/yr] = Pa [kW] × hours/yr ÷ 1000

Unit Conversion Reference

Power Generation Definitions

Theoretical Power (Pth)

The maximum possible power extractable from falling water, assuming 100% efficiency and no losses. Represents the total gravitational potential energy available from the water flow: Pth = ρ × g × Q × h (SI) or γ × Q × h (Imperial).

Practical Power (Pa)

The actual usable power output after accounting for real-world inefficiencies — turbine losses, generator losses, mechanical friction. Typical efficiency ranges from 75–95% depending on turbine type and operating conditions.

Annual Energy Output

The total electrical energy produced over a year: Annual Energy = Practical Power × Operating Hours per Year. Expressed in MWh/yr. For a run-of-river plant operating 4,000 hours/year at 1 MW, that's 4,000 MWh/yr.
